\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Problem setup and notation}

Let $P_t$ denote the daily closing price of a base metal on day $t$, and define the
log return
\begin{equation}
r_t \;=\; \ln P_t - \ln P_{t-1}.
\end{equation}
The forecasting target is the \emph{next-day} log return,
\begin{equation}
y_t \;=\; r_{t+1},
\qquad
\hat{y}_t \;=\; \mathbb{E}\!\left[r_{t+1} \mid \mathcal{F}_t\right],
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{F}_t$ is the information set available at the close of day $t$.
Forecasting returns rather than price levels is deliberate: on levels the
random-walk predictor $\hat{P}_{t+1}=P_t$ attains near-zero relative error and
dominates any accuracy comparison, so a level-based evaluation is uninformative.
Price forecasts are recovered as $\hat{P}_{t+1} = P_t \exp(\hat{y}_t)$.

\subsection{Feature construction}

Two disjoint feature sets are constructed, and the central experimental
manipulation is which of them receives the fundamental signal.

\paragraph{Expert features.} The vector $\bm{x}_t \in \mathbb{R}^{16}$ supplied to
the forecasting models comprises, with lag set $\mathcal{L}=\{1,2,3,4,5,10\}$,
\begin{align}
\text{lagged returns:}\quad & r_{t-\ell+1}, && \ell \in \mathcal{L},\\
\text{lagged magnitudes:}\quad & \lvert r_{t-\ell+1}\rvert, && \ell \in \mathcal{L},\\
\text{realised volatility:}\quad & \sigma^{(w)}_t = \operatorname{sd}\!\big(\{r_s\}_{s=t-w+1}^{t}\big), && w \in \{5,10,20\},\\
\text{trend ratio:}\quad & \rho_t = \frac{\mathrm{MA}_5(P)_t}{\mathrm{MA}_{20}(P)_t} - 1. &&
\end{align}
All components are measurable with respect to $\mathcal{F}_t$.

\paragraph{Gate state.} The router observes a separate market-state vector
$\bm{s}_t$,
\begin{equation}
\bm{s}_t \;=\;
\Big[\, \sigma^{(20)}_t,\;\;
v_t,\;\;
\widehat{H}_t,\;\;
\alpha_t,\;\;
\underbrace{\mathrm{IPI}_t}_{\text{routing conditions only}},\;\;
\bar{e}_{1,t},\dots,\bar{e}_{M,t} \,\Big]^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
where $v_t = \sigma^{(10)}_t / \sigma^{(40)}_t - 1$ is a volatility-regime shift,
$\widehat{H}_t$ the rolling Hurst exponent (Section~\ref{sec:hurst}),
$\alpha_t$ the lag-one autocorrelation of $\lvert r\rvert$ over a 40-day window,
and $\bar{e}_{i,t}$ the trailing mean absolute error of expert $i$
(Equation~\ref{eq:trailerr}).

\subsection{Regime statistics}
\label{sec:hurst}

The Hurst exponent $H$ characterises the self-similarity of a series: the standard
deviation of the lag-$k$ difference scales as $\tau(k)\sim k^{H}$, so
\begin{equation}
\widehat{H}_t \;=\;
\frac{\partial \log \tau(k)}{\partial \log k}\bigg|_{\,\{r_s\}_{s=t-59}^{t}},
\qquad
\tau(k) \;=\; \operatorname{sd}\!\big(P_{s+k}-P_{s}\big),
\end{equation}
estimated by ordinary least squares over $k \in \{2,\dots,19\}$ on a rolling
60-day window. Values near $\tfrac12$ indicate a random walk, $\widehat{H}_t>\tfrac12$
persistence (trending), and $\widehat{H}_t<\tfrac12$ mean reversion. Validated
against known processes, the estimator returns $0.48$ for a simulated random walk,
$0.00$ for white noise, $0.41$ for a mean-reverting and $0.62$ for a trending
AR(1) process.

\subsection{Inventory-pressure index}

The fundamental signal is a composite of the exchange warehouse stock level $I_t$,
its first difference $\Delta I_t$, and the volatility of that difference. With
$z_W(\cdot)$ denoting a rolling $W$-day $z$-score,
\begin{equation}
z_W(u)_t \;=\; \frac{u_t - \operatorname{mean}_W(u)_t}{\operatorname{sd}_W(u)_t},
\end{equation}
the index is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ipi}
\mathrm{IPI}_t \;=\;
-\,\omega_L\, z_W(I)_t
\;+\; \omega_\Delta\, z_W(-\Delta I)_t
\;+\; \omega_V\, z_W\!\big(\operatorname{sd}_W(\Delta I)\big)_t ,
\end{equation}
with $W=20$ and weights $\bm{\omega}=(\omega_L,\omega_\Delta,\omega_V)=(0.5,0.3,0.2)$.
The leading negative sign encodes the economic direction of the theory of storage:
\emph{low} stocks imply \emph{high} physical scarcity. The weights are a design
parameter rather than an estimated quantity; sensitivity to $\bm{\omega}$ is
reported as a robustness check.

\subsection{Expert panel}

$M=4$ heterogeneous one-step-ahead forecasters $f_i:\mathbb{R}^{16}\to\mathbb{R}$
are used, chosen to span distinct hypothesis classes:
\begin{align}
f_1 \;&:\; \text{ridge autoregression, } \; \textstyle f_1(\bm{x}_t)=\beta_0+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\beta_\ell\, r_{t-\ell+1},
&& \hat{\bm\beta}=\arg\min_{\bm\beta}\;\lVert \bm{y}-\bm{X}\bm\beta\rVert_2^2+\alpha\lVert\bm\beta\rVert_2^2 ,\\
f_2 \;&:\; \text{ARIMA}(2,0,1) \text{ fitted on } \{r_t\}, && \text{parameters fixed on the training window},\\
f_3 \;&:\; \text{gradient-boosted regression trees}, && \textstyle f_3(\bm{x})=\sum_{b=1}^{B}\eta\, h_b(\bm{x}),\\
f_4 \;&:\; \text{multilayer perceptron}, && f_4(\bm{x})=\bm{W}_3\,\phi(\bm{W}_2\,\phi(\bm{W}_1\bm{x}+\bm{b}_1)+\bm{b}_2)+b_3 ,
\end{align}
with $\phi=\mathrm{ReLU}$. The target of $f_4$ is standardised before fitting and
the prediction inverse-transformed, without which the $\mathcal{O}(10^{-2})$ scale
of daily returns prevents convergence.

\subsection{Combination layer}

All combiners produce a convex weighting of the panel,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:combine}
\hat{y}_t \;=\; \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_{i,t}\, f_i(\bm{x}_t),
\qquad w_{i,t}\ge 0,\quad \sum_{i=1}^{M} w_{i,t}=1 ,
\end{equation}
and differ only in how $\bm{w}_t$ is produced.

\paragraph{Static combiner (control).} A single time-invariant weight vector
obtained by non-negative least squares on out-of-fold predictions
$\tilde{f}_i$ (Section~\ref{sec:oof}),
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:static}
\bm{w}^{S} \;=\; \frac{\bm{u}}{\bm{1}^{\!\top}\bm{u}},
\qquad
\bm{u} \;=\; \arg\min_{\bm{u}\succeq \bm{0}}\;
\sum_{t}\Big(y_t - \sum_i u_i\,\tilde{f}_i(\bm{x}_t)\Big)^{2}.
\end{equation}
This is the benchmark any state-dependent router must beat; if it cannot, the
\emph{dynamic} component of the architecture is not doing any work.

\paragraph{Gated mixture of experts.} Let $e_{i,t}=f_i(\bm{x}_t)-y_t$ and define
the supervision target as a softmax over scaled negative error,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:oracle}
\bm{a}_t \;=\; \mathrm{softmax}\!\left(-\,\frac{\bm{b}_t}{\tau\,\operatorname{sd}_i(b_{i,t})}\right),
\qquad
b_{i,t} \;=\;
\begin{cases}
\lvert e_{i,t}\rvert & \text{(forward)},\\[2pt]
\bar{e}_{i,t} & \text{(trailing)},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where the division by the \emph{cross-expert} dispersion $\operatorname{sd}_i(b_{i,t})$
renders the temperature $\tau$ dimensionless, and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:trailerr}
\bar{e}_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{1}{L}\sum_{u=1}^{L}\lvert e_{i,\,t-u}\rvert
\end{equation}
is lagged by one step so that $\bm{s}_t$ remains $\mathcal{F}_t$-measurable. The
gate $g_{\bm\theta}$ is trained on centred log-targets and normalised at inference,
\begin{equation}
\bm{w}^{G}_t \;=\; g_{\bm\theta}(\bm{s}_t) \;=\; \mathrm{softmax}\big(m_{\bm\theta}(\bm{s}_t)\big),
\qquad
\bm{\theta} \;=\; \arg\min_{\bm\theta}\sum_t \big\lVert m_{\bm\theta}(\bm{s}_t) - \log \bm{a}_t \big\rVert_2^2 .
\end{equation}
Regressing onto $\log \bm{a}_t$ rather than $\bm{a}_t$ matters: an unconstrained
output layer cannot respect the simplex, and clipping negative outputs can
permanently eliminate an expert.

\subsection{Confidence-thresholded cluster router}

The proposed router addresses a failure mode of Equation~\ref{eq:combine} under a
learned $\bm{w}_t$: existing routers commit at full strength irrespective of how
reliable their own state estimate is. Let $\bm{\pi}_t$ be the row-normalised error
profile,
\begin{equation}
\pi_{i,t} \;=\; \frac{\lvert e_{i,t}\rvert}{\sum_{j}\lvert e_{j,t}\rvert}
\;\in\;\Delta^{M-1},
\end{equation}
which captures \emph{which expert wins} independently of how difficult the day was.
Latent regimes are recovered by $K$-means on $\{\bm{\pi}_t\}$, yielding clusters
$\mathcal{C}_1,\dots,\mathcal{C}_K$ with cluster-conditional weights
$\bm{w}^{(k)}$ obtained by Equation~\ref{eq:static} restricted to $\mathcal{C}_k$.
A probabilistic classifier maps market state to cluster membership,
$q_{k,t} = \Pr(c_t = k \mid \bm{s}_t)$, giving routed weights
\begin{equation}
\bm{w}^{R}_t \;=\; \sum_{k=1}^{K} q_{k,t}\, \bm{w}^{(k)} .
\end{equation}
Router confidence is the top-two margin
$\kappa_t = q_{(1),t} - q_{(2),t} \in [0,1]$, and the final weights shrink toward
the static solution as confidence falls,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ctcr}
\boxed{\;
\bm{w}_t \;=\; \lambda(\kappa_t)\,\bm{w}^{R}_t \;+\; \big(1-\lambda(\kappa_t)\big)\,\bm{w}^{S},
\qquad
\lambda(\kappa) \;=\; \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\kappa-\kappa^{*}}{1-\kappa^{*}},\,0,\,1\right).
\;}
\end{equation}
The threshold $\kappa^{*}$ is \emph{estimated}, not tuned. On a held-out
calibration slice of the training window, define the realised routing benefit
\begin{equation}
\Delta_t \;=\; \big\lvert y_t - \hat{y}^{S}_t \big\rvert - \big\lvert y_t - \hat{y}^{R}_t \big\rvert ,
\end{equation}
fit an isotonic (monotone non-decreasing) regression $\widehat{\Delta}(\kappa)$ of
$\Delta_t$ on $\kappa_t$, and take the zero crossing
\begin{equation}
\kappa^{*} \;=\; \inf\{\kappa : \widehat{\Delta}(\kappa) > 0\} .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Break-even routing accuracy}
\label{sec:breakeven}

The following elementary result gives the accuracy a router must attain before
state-dependent combination is worth performing at all. Define three reference
losses under the panel: $A$, the loss when regime-conditional weights are always
applied correctly; $B$, the loss when they are always applied to the wrong regime;
and $S$, the loss of the static combiner of Equation~\ref{eq:static}.

\begin{proposition}
A router that selects the correct regime with probability $p$, independently of
the loss realisation, attains expected loss $L(p) = pA + (1-p)B$. It improves on
the static combiner if and only if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pstar}
p \;>\; p^{*} \;\equiv\; \frac{B-S}{B-A}.
\end{equation}
Moreover $p^{*} > \tfrac{1}{2}$ whenever $S < \tfrac{1}{2}(A+B)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$L(p) < S \iff pA + (1-p)B < S \iff p(A-B) < S-B$. Since $A<B$, dividing by
$A-B<0$ reverses the inequality, giving $p > (B-S)/(B-A)$. For the second claim,
$p^{*}>\tfrac12 \iff 2(B-S) > B-A \iff A+B > 2S$.
\end{proof}

The practical content of Equation~\ref{eq:pstar} is that $p^{*}$ is \emph{not}
$\tfrac{1}{2}$: a well-fitted static combiner sits far closer to $A$ than to $B$,
which pushes the break-even well above a coin flip. Routing is therefore a wager
that must clear a high bar before it pays, and reporting $p^{*}$ alongside a
measured classifier accuracy makes a negative routing result interpretable rather
than merely disappointing.

\subsection{Out-of-fold construction of combiner inputs}
\label{sec:oof}

Combiner weights must be learned from expert predictions on data the experts did
not fit, or the combiner rewards memorisation. Within each training window
$\mathcal{T}$, contiguous forward-only blocks $\mathcal{B}_1,\dots,\mathcal{B}_J$
are formed after a warm-up, and
\begin{equation}
\tilde{f}_i(\bm{x}_t) \;=\; f_i^{\,(<\!j)}(\bm{x}_t),
\qquad t \in \mathcal{B}_j ,
\end{equation}
where $f_i^{\,(<\!j)}$ is expert $i$ fitted on $\{s \in \mathcal{T} : s < \min \mathcal{B}_j\}$.
Blocks are contiguous and forward-only; shuffled $k$-fold would let an expert
train on the future of its own evaluation rows.

\subsection{Evaluation}

Let $\hat{y}^{\mathrm{RW}}_t = 0$ denote the random-walk benchmark on returns.
Reported statistics are
\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE} &= \frac{1}{n}\sum_t \lvert y_t-\hat{y}_t\rvert,
&
\mathrm{RelMAE} &= \frac{\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{y})}{\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{y}^{\mathrm{RW}})},
&
\mathrm{MASE} &= \frac{\mathrm{MAE}(\hat{y})}{\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}-1}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{T}}\lvert y_t-y_{t-1}\rvert}.
\end{align}
Directional accuracy is computed with explicit abstention: timesteps where
$\lvert \hat{y}_t\rvert \le \epsilon$ express no directional view and are excluded
from the accuracy while the decision rate is reported separately, avoiding the
degenerate scoring of a zero-valued benchmark.

Equal predictive accuracy between two forecasts is tested by
Diebold--Mariano \cite{diebold1995}. With loss differential
$d_t = \lvert e^{A}_t\rvert^{k} - \lvert e^{B}_t\rvert^{k}$,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{DM} \;=\; \frac{\bar{d}}{\sqrt{\hat{\gamma}_0/n}}
\cdot
\sqrt{\frac{n+1-2h+h(h-1)/n}{n}},
\qquad
\hat{\gamma}_0 = \frac{1}{n}\sum_t (d_t-\bar{d})^2 ,
\end{equation}
including the Harvey--Leybourne--Newbold small-sample correction, referred to a
$t_{n-1}$ distribution.

All results use expanding-window walk-forward evaluation: for fold
$k=1,\dots,K$, all components are fitted on $\{1,\dots,T_k\}$ and evaluated on
$\{T_k+1,\dots,T_{k+1}\}$, with predictions pooled across folds before metrics are
computed.

\subsection{Experimental design}

The central manipulation places $\mathrm{IPI}_t$ (Equation~\ref{eq:ipi}) in the
expert features, the gate state, both, or neither:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\hline
& gate without $\mathrm{IPI}$ & gate with $\mathrm{IPI}$ \\
\hline
experts without $\mathrm{IPI}$ & (a) neither & (c) routing only \\
experts with $\mathrm{IPI}$ & (b) representation & (d) both \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
All four conditions share one feature frame, one set of folds and one listwise
deletion, so they differ only in which columns each component may read. Condition
(c) isolates the hypothesis that a fundamental signal is more valuable for
selecting \emph{among} forecasters than for improving any single forecast.
